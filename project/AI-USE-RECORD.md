# AI Use Record

## 1. Did I use any generative AI?

Yes. I used generative AI tools for parts of my Requirements Engineering project: research, brainstorming, understanding concepts, proofreading, fact-checking and reviewing my work. I used them to help me learn. The work, understanding and decisions in the project are mine.

## 2. What tools did I use?

- ChatGPT (OpenAI): October–November 2026.
- Claude (Anthropic): October–November 2026.
- Google Search and YouTube: for researching Requirements Engineering concepts and learning how to use GitHub and draw.io. These are not generative AI tools.
- Lecture notes and practical labs: my main resources for learning the module content and applying it to the project. These are provided with the module and are not AI tools.

## 3. How did I use these tools?

### ChatGPT (OpenAI)

- Brainstorming ideas and organising the structure and sections of the project.
- Understanding the Requirements Engineering concepts covered in the module.
- Fact-checking my research and clarifying concepts I was unsure about.
- Spelling, grammar and proofreading.
- Exploring possible approaches to requirements analysis and modelling, including use case and activity diagrams.
- I used AI-generated diagram drafts as visual references to see how the diagrams could be structured. I then recreated the diagrams myself in draw.io, changing, correcting and improving them so they matched my project.

### Claude (Anthropic)

- Proofreading my GitHub repository for spelling mistakes, inconsistent file names and inconsistent requirement IDs.
- Comparing my repository against the project brief and report template to find information that might be missing and areas that needed more work.
- Understanding the assessment expectations and seeing where my work could be improved.
- I checked each suggestion before deciding whether to use it.

### Google Search and YouTube

- Researching Requirements Engineering concepts.
- Learning how to use GitHub and draw.io through tutorials and other resources.
- Cross-checking information and building my understanding of the subject.

### Lecture notes and practical labs

- My main resources for the material taught in the module.
- Applying the concepts, methods and techniques from lectures and practicals to my project.

I checked all the information and suggestions I got from research and AI tools, and decided for myself what was relevant, appropriate and accurate for the project.

## 4. What is my own work?

The project is my own work, built on what I learned in lectures, practical labs and my own research.

I researched the scenario, worked out the requirements, analysed the information and decided how the proposed system should work.

AI tools helped me explore ideas and showed me examples of possible diagrams and approaches, but I did not copy and submit their outputs. I used the AI-assisted diagram drafts as references, then recreated the diagrams myself in draw.io and adjusted their structure and content and fixed inaccuracies.

I organised and maintained my GitHub repository myself, created and edited the project files, reviewed the requirements and decided on the final content.

I treated AI suggestions as guidance and checked them against my research, the lecture materials, the practical labs and the project brief. Where something was uncertain, I tried to keep confirmed requirements separate from assumptions and open questions, so that I didn't present anything unconfirmed as fact.

I take full responsibility for the final requirements, analysis, modelling decisions, documentation and submission.
