# Week 2 — Stakeholders and Requirements Elicitation

## 1. Introduction

The aim of this stage is to identify the people involved in the Student Support Appointment System and understand their needs. More information is needed before deciding exactly how the system should work.

## 2. Stakeholders

| Stakeholder | Role | Direct User? | Possible Needs or Concerns |
|---|---|---|---|
| Students | People seeking support | Yes, if they use the system | Finding suitable appointments and keeping personal information private |
| Student support staff | Provide support to students | Yes, if they use the system | Viewing and managing appointments |
| Support administrators | May organise appointment schedules | To be confirmed | Keeping appointment records organised |
| College management | Oversees student support services | Not necessarily | Ensuring the service meets students' needs |
| IT support staff | May maintain the system | Possibly | Keeping the system reliable and secure |
| Data protection representative | Advises on personal information handling | Not necessarily | Protecting confidential student information |

The exact responsibilities of these stakeholders still need to be confirmed.

## 3. Stakeholder Needs and Concerns

### Students

- Need to find out what support is available.
- May need appointments at suitable times.
- May be concerned about the privacy of their information.

### Student support staff

- Need to view scheduled appointments.
- Need to organise their time.
- May be concerned about missed appointments or scheduling conflicts.

### Support administrators

- May need to organise appointment records.
- Need clear information about appointment times.
- May be concerned about incorrect or conflicting bookings.

### College management

- Needs the service to support students effectively.
- May be concerned about staff workload and available resources.

### IT support staff

- May need to maintain the system.
- May be concerned about technical problems and unauthorised access.

### Data protection representative

- May need to ensure that personal information is handled appropriately.
- May be concerned about confidential information being exposed.

## 4. Unknowns

| Unknown | Who Could Provide the Information? |
|---|---|
| What types of student support can be booked? | Student support staff or college management |
| Who can create, change or cancel appointments? | Support administrators or service management |
| Can students cancel or change their own appointments? | Students and student support staff |
| What information must be recorded for each appointment? | Student support staff and the relevant privacy representative |

These questions need answers from suitable sources rather than assumptions.

## 5. Information Sources

Possible sources of information include:

- Students who use or may need student support.
- Student support staff who provide appointments.
- Administrators who organise appointment schedules.
- College policies relating to student support and personal information.
- The official project specification and any clarification provided by the lecturer.

## 6. Elicitation Questions

These questions could be asked during stakeholder interviews.

1. How are student support appointments currently arranged?
2. What problems do students experience when trying to arrange an appointment?
3. What happens when a student needs to cancel or change an appointment?
4. What information needs to be recorded when an appointment is booked?
5. Who should be allowed to view or change appointment information?

These questions aim to understand the current process without assuming a particular solution.
